What leaves your browser.

Very little. The tutorial uses public posts. Your own research data never belongs on this site; run it in your own environment.

The tutorial and the lab

Charts and data load as public files. In the lab, what you type (your topic name, your theme choices) stays in the page. When you ask the live model, only the ID of a recorded topic or an archived public post is sent to the site's server, which looks up the public text and sends it to OpenAI with the frozen codebook. The server doesn't accept arbitrary text or custom prompts.

Your running “code five” score is kept in this browser's local storage and nowhere else. Clearing site data resets it.

The Ask guide

When you ask the guide a question, your question, the recent conversation and relevant excerpts from the public project documents go to OpenAI. The conversation lives in page memory and disappears when you reload. Don't paste private research records or credentials into it. The guide is an AI that explains public materials; it isn't a reviewer and can't approve anyone's science.

Human coding records

The tutorial publishes aggregate results: the codebook, agreement figures and confusion counts. Individual human labels, adjudication notes and post-level predictions are kept private.

Limits on live calls

The lab and the guide share 200 calls per browser session and 600 per network per day (UTC). Asking the model 20 times for a name uses 20 calls; coding five posts uses 5. Site-wide spending is capped at $20 per day and $620 per calendar month. These are shared allowances, not a promise of availability. Budgets are reserved before each request and fail closed if accounting is unavailable.

Credentials and retention

The OpenAI key stays on the server. Signed session cookies expire after one day. The quota service stores daily keyed digests of IP addresses (never raw addresses), counters, and short response receipts that expire after 30 minutes; it doesn't store raw prompts. Requests to OpenAI use store: false, which is not a claim of zero retention by the provider.
