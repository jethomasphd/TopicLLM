The original research

TopicLLM grew out of dissertation research at The University of Texas at Austin. These documents examine alcohol-related social media during the COVID-19 pandemic and are preserved unchanged. The Trump tutorial is a separate application of the workflow on new data.

Author's note

Dear researcher,

TopicLLM began with a question in my dissertation research at The University of Texas at Austin: what were alcohol companies posting about on social media during the COVID-19 pandemic?

Topic models can find patterns across thousands of texts. Qualitative research explains what those patterns mean. We wanted a clear computational workflow that brought these approaches together and kept researchers responsible for interpretation. The Theme and Topic framework (Gillies, Murthy, Brenton & Olaniyan, 2022) informed this work.

The dissertation used this approach across two studies. Study 1 identified themes in alcohol companies' Twitter posts. Study 2 applied a selected theme to a larger corpus of general users' posts and examined its relationship to potential exposure to brand messages. The software develops the topic-to-prevalence workflow for reuse; it does not implement every analysis in the dissertation.

The tutorial applies the method to a separate, familiar public archive, Donald Trump's Twitter account, so that anyone can follow every step. It is a walkthrough of the method on new data, not a reproduction of the dissertation's findings.

My hope in releasing the code, data and papers is that others will test, challenge and extend this work. Whether you are an undergraduate or a tenured professor, I am happy to help you get started.

Thank you for your time, curiosity and care.
