Coherence-Optimized Topic Modeling with Democratic Large Language Model Interpretation: A Human-in-the-Loop Pipeline for Public Health Surveillance of Social Media

Jacob E. Thomas¹, Dhiraj Murthy², Miguel Pinedo¹, Alexandra Loukas¹, Keryn E. Pasch¹

¹Department of Kinesiology and Health Education, The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, TX, USA

²School of Journalism and Media, The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, TX, USA

Correspondence: jethomasphd@gmail.com

Abstract

TopicLLM converts collections of short texts into interpretable themes and theme-prevalence estimates through coherence-guided BERTopic discovery, repeated large language model naming, researcher-led synthesis, benchmarked classification and period comparisons. This paper describes the dissertation methodology and links each step to the maintained Python implementation and website. The public demonstration uses original Study 1 alcohol-brand data: a 5,872-record full corpus and a 704-text lockdown discovery input. It is an illustrative modern rerun, not an exact replay of the historical outputs. Topic models and LLMs include probabilistic steps; changing random seeds, models or software versions can change groupings and labels. The intended workflow is agent-executed and researcher-reviewed: an agent performs computation and retains inputs, settings and outputs, while researchers approve interpretation and validation. The dissertation remains the source for historical findings.

1. Purpose and source hierarchy

The purpose of this workflow is to make a large text corpus analyzable without hiding the interpretive decisions between statistical clusters and meaningful themes. Topics are patterns estimated by a model. Labels describe those patterns. Themes are categories justified by researchers. A classifier then applies the agreed taxonomy to documents, providing the numerators for prevalence estimates.

The authoritative account is Thomas (2025), especially Chapter 4, pages 148–179, Chapter 5, pages 180–205, and Appendices 1–3, pages 230–238. The dissertation is preserved without alteration. The existing study preprints are reflowed editions whose body text was checked against the chapters; the original PDF remains the source for exact wording, tables, figures and pagination. The maintained implementation and original-data demonstration are separately identified.

This release distinguishes findings reported in the dissertation, a modern illustrative rerun on original Study 1 data, and new researcher-run analyses. The demonstration makes the methodology and evidence inspectable without requiring identical historical outputs. Agents execute the documented workflow; researchers review interpretation and validation.

2. The five-stage method

2.1 Corpus design and preprocessing

Define the population, collection mechanism, inclusion rules and time boundaries before modeling. Retain an immutable source copy, stable document identifiers and a record of exclusions. The study sample comprised 5,872 alcohol-brand tweets from 11 companies between January 2019 and July 2021 (dissertation pp. 154–155). The recovered archive also contains a 704-text lockdown modeling file covering March 25–May 31, 2020. This released input supplies the modern discovery illustration; its preparation and relationship to the full corpus are documented in Section 4.

The printed Python implementation removes non-alphabetic characters, collapses whitespace, lowercases text, tokenizes with NLTK (Bird et al., 2009), removes English and custom domain stopwords, and applies part-of-speech-informed WordNet lemmatization. Domain stopwords include brand terms and temporal words. Document the exact list and operation order; they change the vocabulary and the geometry of the subsequent analysis. Blank or unusable records require an explicit disposition rather than being embedded as the words “null” or “undefined.”

2.2 Discover topics

The historical model uses all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence embeddings (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019; Wang et al., 2020), cosine-distance UMAP (McInnes et al., 2018), Euclidean-distance HDBSCAN (Campello et al., 2013) with excess-of-mass cluster selection, and BERTopic’s class-based TF-IDF representation and topic reduction (Grootendorst, 2022). The random search draws 50 parameter configurations. UMAP neighbors are sampled from 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35; components from 3 through 10; minimum distance from 0.01, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.5; HDBSCAN minimum cluster size from 5 through 35 in increments of five. Each draw considers maximum-topic settings 2 through 25: 1,200 planned candidate fits.

Gensim’s cv coherence is the selection objective (Röder et al., 2015). Record attempted, successful and failed candidates separately. A requested maximum-topic setting is not the observed number of non-outlier topics. Coherence measures word association, not substantive validity, and can favor narrow lexical patterns in narrow or repetitive text. Inspect outlier share, representative documents and sensitivity to preprocessing as well as the selected score.

The dissertation reports iteration 25, UMAP neighbors 15, components 4 and minimum distance 0.01, HDBSCAN minimum cluster size 20, maximum-topic setting 23, cv 0.768, and seven observed topics (p. 163). The maintained implementation retains the fitted candidate that earned the winning score, together with its configuration, assignments and provenance. This preserves the connection between the reported search score and the model used downstream. For each sampled configuration, the maintained search fits the embedding reduction and clustering once, then evaluates topic reductions across ceilings. The archival supplement refits for each ceiling; refit_per_solution=true retains that option. This implementation choice is recorded in the run configuration and may affect results. The modern run uses the recovered original Study 1 input, with its present software environment and declared seed. It illustrates the method without claiming identical historical outputs.

2.3 Name topics with repeated LLM queries

The historical naming protocol makes 5,000 separate GPT-4o calls per topic, each with temperature 0.5, a maximum of 15 output tokens and one completion. It adopts the most frequent interpretation (p. 159 and Appendix 2). Seven topics imply 35,000 naming calls before retries. These are repeated draws from one model, not independent human annotators or an ensemble of independently trained models.

The maintained implementation retains raw ballots and normalizes surrounding quotes, case and terminal periods for tallying. The denominator is the number of valid votes, not attempted calls. Failed, empty or truncated responses must not become labels. Preserve the normalization rule, requested and completed counts, model identifier, decoding parameters and any stopping rule. Ties require explicit inspection. A fragmented vote can indicate synonymous wording as well as semantic ambiguity; a concentrated vote does not establish truth or remove shared model bias.

The optional leader-share stopping rule ends voting after a declared minimum number of votes when the cumulative leading share exceeds a threshold. It is a cost heuristic introduced by the maintained implementation. It is not a demonstrated convergence test and is not the fixed-budget historical protocol.

2.4 Synthesize themes through researcher review

Researchers review representative words, sampled documents, model labels and relevant literature, following the Theme and Topic approach (Gillies et al., 2022). The primary researcher checks the proposed interpretations; a senior researcher reviews and critiques them; discussion resolves differences (dissertation pp. 159–160). The source does not establish blinded independent annotation. Record reviewers, evidence, decisions, exclusions and the final topic-to-theme map.

In the historical study, seven topics became five themes. The two topics concerning virtual bar tours and social engagement became Social Media Promotions. The virtual draft party and home sports challenge topics became Sports. Delivery, restaurant support and charity gaming each contributed one topic to their respective theme. Topic IDs shown in the illustrative walkthrough are display identifiers, not recovered original model IDs.

2.5 Validate and classify documents

The dissertation describes Boolean and LLM classification pilots and reports selecting GPT-4o after greater than 85% agreement with 250 human-labeled classifications (pp. 136–137 and 161). The original benchmark labels and confusion matrix are not supplied. The historical classification prompt uses one completion, temperature 0.5 and a 30-token maximum. The maintained gateway allows 120 output tokens to avoid truncating long theme names; this change must be reported when used.

For new research, separate prompt development from held-out evaluation. Report positive support, precision, sensitivity (recall), confusion counts and overall agreement for every theme. Overall accuracy alone can conceal failure on a rare category. For example, the dissertation reports 536 alcohol-delivery/isolation classifications among 486,786 user-generated tweets (p. 192); a detector that always predicts absence would be approximately 99.89% accurate while detecting no positives. The maintained gate requires agreement strictly above the chosen threshold and positive benchmark support with nonzero sensitivity for every theme. These minimum checks do not establish that error rates are acceptable for a particular research question.

The maintained Boolean comparator counts dictionary matches and requires at least two hits; it is a transparent baseline, not a reconstruction of the original undisclosed Boolean dictionaries. The LLM assigns one agreed theme or the explicit residual option. Accept only a single unambiguous option. Refusals, multiple choices, failed calls and missing text are unresolved measurements, not evidence that a document belongs to no theme. Do not estimate prevalence until these cases have been resolved.

2.6 Estimate prevalence and compare periods

For theme t in period p, prevalence is k(t,p) / n(p): the number of classified documents assigned to that theme divided by all eligible, successfully classified documents in the period. The historical periods are January 2019–January 2020, March–May 2020 and June 2020–July 2021. February 2020 is outside these comparison periods. Period definitions must be non-overlapping, dates valid, and denominators explicitly reported.

Use a pooled, two-sided, two-proportion z-test for each of the three pairwise comparisons per theme, matching the reported Stata 17 design. With five themes, the Bonferroni threshold is 0.05 / 15 = 0.003333…. This implementation defines positive z as an increase from the earlier listed period to the later one. Report counts, denominators, percentage-point differences, relative changes, z, p and the comparison family. Zero baseline prevalence makes relative change undefined; zero pooled variance makes the z-test not estimable. Sparse cells need appropriate methodological review.

These tests condition on the assigned labels and the independent-binomial approximation. They do not propagate classification uncertainty or account for repeated posts from the same brand and temporal dependence. The estimates describe the sampled corpus, and the comparisons do not establish a causal effect of the pandemic.

3. Exact prompts and implementation contract

Human-readable templates appear below. Braces denote substituted values, not literal prompt text. The machine-readable protocol.json preserves the exact concatenation, spaces, output limits and provenance categories. Pin the actual model identifier and preserve provider-returned metadata where available. The historical source identifies GPT-4o but does not establish the precise dated snapshot used.

Naming system
You are an expert at naming topics.

Naming user
The following words represent a topic derived from {domain}: {words}. Please provide a concise, meaningful topic name that clearly summarizes these words.
Classification system
You are an expert at classifying text into themes.

Classification user
Classify the following text into one of the following {N} themes: {numbered_options}.Text: {document}.Respond with verbatim classification using only the specified {N} options.

Adapting the workflow to another domain requires more than changing one prompt clause. Reconsider corpus sampling, preprocessing and stopwords, topic interpretation, theme definitions, dictionaries, human benchmarks, period boundaries and inferential assumptions. Retain the residual category, and document how ambiguous or multi-theme documents are handled under the single-label design.

Checkpoint reuse must be bound to a fingerprint of ordered inputs, topic words, theme options, prompts, model and decoding settings. Reordering a corpus or changing a topic’s words invalidates prior assignments even when the row count or topic ID is unchanged. Keep raw responses separate from accepted labels and expose all unresolved outcomes.

4. Original Study 1 demonstration

The author-authorized public release contains original Study 1 brand content. The full dataset has 5,872 records from 11 alcohol brands over January 2019–July 2021. One record has blank tweet text; it remains in the full release, while the nonempty analysis input has 5,871 records. Preparation reproduces the archived cohort by removing 267 exact-text duplicates, 20 Topo Chico records and 15 August 2021 records from 6,174 collected records. Research fields and stable IDs are released; collection personnel and administrative fields are omitted. Study 2 user data are not included.

The separately prepared lockdown branch covers March 25–May 31, 2020: 742 collected records become 705 after within-window text deduplication, and 704 after removal of one blank text. This archived input supplies the modern discovery illustration. Filtering before versus after global deduplication can retain different records; the public manifest documents the branch explicitly. The discovery file preserves archived modeling text, including encoding artifacts, and supplies readable collection transcription separately for display.

The recorded maintained search evaluated 50 parameter draws across ceilings 2–25. Of 1,200 candidate records, 1,151 were scored and 49 had too few topics. The selected solution had five statistical topics, cv = 0.7803, and 433 of 704 documents designated as outliers. These five topics are not asserted to equal the dissertation's five researcher-defined themes. At the reported historical parameter settings, seeds 42, 43 and 44 produced 9, 10 and 9 topics; repeating seed 42 reproduced all assignments in the recorded environment.

Topic models and LLMs include probabilistic steps. A fresh run can group or label some posts differently, especially when the random seed, model, or software version changes. The demonstration's purpose is to make the methodology tangible using original data, not to impose the historical topic count. Published prevalence remains labeled as reported; live examples are new illustrative responses. No final human benchmark or historical ballot log is asserted by this release.

The browser walkthrough explains each stage. Live naming collects small independent vote samples, and live classification operates on released Study 1 posts. The optional browser sandbox approximates discovery with JavaScript components and mock naming; the Python BERTopic implementation is the research execution path. The package does not implement LIWC sentiment analysis or Study 2's exposure-index and GSEM analysis.

5. Reproducible execution with agents and researchers

The website introduces the method; the public repository and Python package are the execution environment. A researcher supplies the question and reviews the scientific decisions. An agent reads AGENTS.md, REPRODUCE.md and protocol.json, checks the original inputs, records the environment, plans costs, runs the stages and assembles their evidence. The default quickcheck verifies the released Study 1 files and reconstructs the cohort without model calls. The study1-demo profile runs discovery on the archived 704-text input.

The maintained configuration distinguishes discovery_input_csv from input_csv. The former supplies topic discovery; the latter supplies the nonempty corpus for subsequent classification and period estimation. For a new research corpus, explicitly declare the sampling frame, discovery subset, stopwords, themes, independent benchmark and period boundaries. Approval records bind both input files and the current synthesis evidence. Paid stages require an explicit budget and private provider credentials.

For research, two actual researchers review and approve the theme map before classification, and the classifier must pass the declared benchmark gate. The interactive public examples illustrate those operations without pretending that a demonstration label is a validated research finding. Legacy generated fixtures remain explicitly identified software tests; they are not the default data or evidence for current scientific claims.

Retain input hashes, exact dependency and model revisions, seeds, successful and failed candidates, topic sizes and outliers, raw naming responses, theme decisions, benchmark support and per-theme performance, classifications, exclusions and period denominators. Fixed seeds improve repeatability within a recorded environment; they do not guarantee historical equivalence across changed models, dependencies or hardware.

The Cloudflare Worker keeps credentials server-side and accepts only released Study 1 IDs for live model examples. The conversational guide answers from public research and repository documentation. Both share atomic spending and usage limits. Arbitrary uploaded research records are processed only in the researcher's approved environment. The website offers a transparent demonstration; a fork provides the auditable path to a new analysis.

6. Source provenance and interpretation

The dissertation governs historical claims and remains unchanged. Original papers and archival supplements are protected by checksums. This maintained methods edition explains the same scientific sequence and its current implementation, with an original-data demonstration. The recovered dataset and summary workbook agree on the cohort counts; the source PDF's small brand/count table-ordering differences are retained in the original and identified in the dataset provenance.

Transparency means readers can inspect the inputs, preparation, settings, decisions and outputs. Reproducibility here means another agent or researcher can rerun the documented workflow and assess the resulting evidence. It is not a promise that every probabilistic output will be identical to the dissertation. Researchers remain responsible for interpretation, validation and the scope of scientific claims.
