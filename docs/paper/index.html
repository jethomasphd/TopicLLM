TopicLLM: A Human-Guided, Agent-Executable Workflow for Topic Interpretation and Prevalence Analysis

Methodology transfer from dissertation research to the Trump Twitter archive

Jacob E. Thomas¹, Dhiraj Murthy², Miguel Pinedo¹, Alexandra Loukas¹, Keryn E. Pasch¹

¹Department of Kinesiology and Health Education, The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, TX, USA

²School of Journalism and Media, The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, TX, USA

Correspondence: jethomasphd@gmail.com

Abstract

Computational topic models can organize large text collections, but scientific interpretation requires an explicit connection between statistical groups, researcher-defined concepts and validated measurements. TopicLLM provides a five-stage workflow: coherence-guided topic discovery; repeated large language model (LLM) naming; researcher-led theme synthesis; independently benchmarked classification; and prevalence estimation. Developed during dissertation research on alcohol-related social media communication at The University of Texas at Austin, the method is released as an executable Python protocol supported by a visual tutorial and agent-readable instructions. This paper transfers that methodology to a new, publicly available corpus of 34,416 posts from the Donald Trump account, spanning June 16, 2015 through January 8, 2021. The original dissertation and two study papers document prior applications; their datasets are not distributed. We specify the new corpus selection, preprocessing, hyperparameter search, prompts, review procedure, validation gates, statistical estimands and reproducibility artifacts. The tutorial exposes measured discovery and interactive naming, synthesis and classification exercises without presenting unreviewed outputs as validated findings. Coding agents execute maintained scripts and preserve evidence; researchers retain responsibility for the question, interpretation, annotations and inference. The contribution is an inspectable implementation and worked method-transfer example, not a new substantive account of political communication or a claim of independent scientific validation.

Keywords: topic modeling; BERTopic; large language models; human-in-the-loop; social media; methodology transfer; reproducible research; research agents

1. Introduction

TopicLLM originated in dissertation research at The University of Texas at Austin on alcohol companies’ social media communication during the COVID-19 pandemic (Thomas, 2025). The substantive question was what alcohol companies communicated during the pandemic and how the prevalence of those messages changed. Topic models can organize thousands of posts, but a statistical group does not explain its public health meaning. Qualitative interpretation supplies that context, while requiring a transparent way to connect interpretation to corpus-scale measurement. The Theme and Topic framework integrates topic modeling with qualitative research (Gillies et al., 2022). TopicLLM builds on that approach with an explicit, inspectable sequence linking transformer-based discovery, repeated LLM naming, researcher-led synthesis, validated classification and prevalence estimation. It is offered as a starting implementation for researchers to evaluate and adapt.

Study 1 identified themes in commercial alcohol-brand posts; Study 2 applied a selected theme to a larger corpus of general users’ posts and examined potential exposure to brand messages. The maintained package focuses on the Study 1 topic-to-prevalence workflow. Historical references to Twitter and tweets refer to the platform now called X. Applications to other social media or text corpora require their own sampling rationale, preprocessing, interpretive framework and classifier validation.

The purpose of this workflow is to make a large text corpus analyzable without hiding the interpretive decisions between statistical clusters and meaningful themes. Topics are patterns estimated by a model. Labels describe those patterns. Themes are categories justified by researchers through contextual interpretation, drawing on the qualitative principles discussed in the dissertation (Braun & Clarke, 2006; Nowell et al., 2017). A classifier then applies the agreed taxonomy to documents, providing the numerators for prevalence estimates. Here, a corpus is the collection of texts; a topic is a group inferred from patterns in their language; a theme is a category defined through researcher interpretation; and prevalence is the proportion of eligible texts assigned to a theme. “Democratic” naming denotes a frequency tally of repeated model responses, not consensus among human researchers.

Thomas (2025), Chapter 4 and Appendices 1–3, supplies the historical methods and findings. The present paper specifies the topic-to-prevalence workflow and identifies operational additions in the maintained implementation, including persisted model artifacts, budget controls and explicit researcher and validation checkpoints. It does not require the reader to use the website to understand the method.

This release separates the historical research record from the hands-on tutorial. The dissertation and two unchanged study papers show the research in action. A public Trump account archive provides one new corpus for learning and executing the methodology. Different substantive findings are expected on different data. The purpose is to reproduce an explicit sequence of methodological operations, not to recover the dissertation’s themes from another domain. No dissertation research dataset is included.

2. The five-stage method

2.1 Corpus design and preprocessing

Define the question, population, collection mechanism, unit of analysis, inclusion rules and time boundaries before modeling. Retain an immutable source copy, stable document identifiers and exclusions. The historical Study 1 analyzed commercial alcohol-brand posts collected through a standardized research protocol; the original collection and institutional determination are documented in the dissertation (Thomas, 2025, pp. 154–155). Those design decisions and permissions do not automatically transfer to a new corpus. Section 4 specifies the public tutorial’s different sampling frame and unit: an archived posting event from one account.

Appendix 1 of the dissertation uses non-alphabetic character removal, whitespace normalization, lowercasing, NLTK tokenization (Bird et al., 2009), English and custom stopwords, and part-of-speech-informed WordNet lemmatization. The historical implementation used Python 3.9.12 (Python Software Foundation, 2022). The maintained release preserves this core order and records the actual environment. For the Trump tutorial, it first decodes HTML entities and removes URLs and @handles from the modeling copy. This declared adaptation prevents link fragments and account mentions from dominating lexical topics. Hashtag words remain after punctuation removal. The raw text is preserved unchanged. English stopwords are combined with six explicit platform/account tokens: rt, https, http, co, amp and realdonaldtrump. No political issue, candidate or opponent vocabulary is removed.

Texts with no remaining tokens are excluded from discovery and written to an exclusion file with their original IDs; they remain in the source corpus. The selected tutorial has no blank raw text. Excluding an empty modeled text from clustering is different from deleting a source record. Classification, if approved and validated, operates on the original text of the same corpus. Researchers should inspect the effects of tokenization, lemmatization, negation removal and stopwords on their question and document any sensitivity analysis as a separately configured run.

2.2 Discover topics

BERTopic was chosen for flexible topic discovery and coherence-guided exploration, consistent with the comparison discussed in the dissertation (Egger & Yu, 2022). The model first converts each preprocessed text into a numeric sentence embedding using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019; Wang et al., 2020). Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) reduces these vectors to a smaller space using cosine distance (McInnes et al., 2018). Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) then groups nearby observations using Euclidean distance and excess-of-mass cluster selection (Campello et al., 2013). Texts not assigned to a cluster remain outliers, labeled −1. BERTopic represents each cluster with characteristic words using class-based term frequency–inverse document frequency (TF-IDF), then can merge topics to evaluate a requested topic ceiling (Grootendorst, 2022). The representation uses CountVectorizer with the same stopword set and ClassTfidfTransformer; class-based weighting is implemented by BERTopic, building on the TF-IDF principle described by Ramos (2003).

The random search draws 50 parameter configurations (Bergstra & Bengio, 2012). UMAP neighbors are sampled from 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35; components from 3 through 10; minimum distance from 0.01, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.5; HDBSCAN minimum cluster size from 5 through 35 in increments of five. Each draw considers maximum-topic settings 2 through 25: 1,200 planned candidate evaluations. The maintained fit-once/reduce implementation makes 50 base fits; the archival refit-per-ceiling option makes up to 1,200 fits.

Gensim’s cv coherence, a measure of how strongly a topic’s representative words occur together in the corpus, is the selection objective (Röder et al., 2015). The dissertation motivates coherence through research on human interpretability (Newman et al., 2010) and a BERTopic application to e-cigarette social media content (Lee et al., 2024). Record attempted, successful and failed candidates separately. A requested maximum-topic setting is not the observed number of non-outlier topics. Coherence measures word association, not substantive validity, and can favor narrow lexical patterns in narrow or repetitive text. Inspect outlier share, representative documents and sensitivity to preprocessing as well as the selected score.

The dissertation reports iteration 25, UMAP neighbors 15, components 4 and minimum distance 0.01, HDBSCAN minimum cluster size 20, maximum-topic setting 23, cv 0.768, and seven observed topics (p. 163). The maintained implementation retains the fitted candidate that earned the winning score, together with its configuration, assignments and provenance. This preserves the connection between the reported search score and the model used downstream. For each sampled configuration, the maintained search fits the embedding reduction and clustering once, then evaluates topic reductions across ceilings. The archival supplement refits for each ceiling; refit_per_solution=true retains that option. This implementation choice is recorded in the run configuration and may affect results. The new tutorial uses the Trump corpus with a recorded environment and declared seed. Exact repeated word arrays on the same corpus reuse cached coherence scores; every candidate remains in the record. This caching changes computation time, not the scoring definition.

2.3 Name topics with repeated LLM queries

The dissertation draws on emerging work on LLM topic labeling (Li et al., 2023). Its naming protocol makes 5,000 separate GPT-4o calls per topic (OpenAI, 2024), each with temperature 0.5, a maximum of 15 output tokens and one completion. It adopts the most frequent interpretation (p. 159 and Appendix 2). Seven topics imply 35,000 naming calls before retries. These are repeated draws from one model, not independent human annotators or an ensemble of independently trained models.

The maintained implementation retains raw ballots and normalizes surrounding quotes, case and terminal periods for tallying. The denominator is the number of valid votes, not attempted calls. Failed, empty or truncated responses must not become labels. Preserve the normalization rule, requested and completed counts, model identifier, decoding parameters and any stopping rule. Ties require explicit inspection. A fragmented vote can indicate synonymous wording as well as semantic ambiguity; a concentrated vote does not establish truth or remove shared model bias.

The optional leader-share stopping rule ends voting after a declared minimum number of votes when the cumulative leading share reaches a threshold (defaults: 500 votes and 0.60). It is disabled by default. It is a cost heuristic introduced by the maintained implementation. It is not a demonstrated convergence test and is not the fixed-budget historical protocol.

2.4 Synthesize themes through researcher review

Two researchers review representative words, sampled documents, model labels and relevant literature, following the Theme and Topic approach and related applied work (Gillies et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2024). In Study 1, interpretation was informed by COVID-19 alcohol-marketing studies from Australia and New Zealand (Martino et al., 2021; Gerritsen et al., 2021). They examine whether labels fit the posts and group topics by shared meaning and context. The primary researcher checks the proposed interpretations; a senior researcher reviews and critiques them; discussion resolves differences (dissertation pp. 159–160). This is a collaborative interpretive procedure rather than blinded independent annotation. Record reviewers, evidence, decisions, exclusions and the final topic-to-theme map.

In the historical study, seven topics became five themes. For example, the words “restaurant,” “donate,” “support” and “worker” supported the topic name “COVID-19 Restaurant Support and Donation Initiatives”; review of the associated posts informed the theme “Restaurant Support” (dissertation Table 2). The word list proposes a pattern, and the documents and literature establish its interpretation. The two topics concerning virtual bar tours and social engagement became Social Media Promotions. The virtual draft party and home sports challenge topics became Sports. Delivery, restaurant support and charity gaming each contributed one topic to their respective theme. These historical findings belong to the alcohol-brand study; they are not a taxonomy or topic-count target for the Trump corpus.

For the Trump corpus, inspect language in its temporal and political context, including quoted speech and retweets. A label about “media,” for example, could describe reporting, criticism of journalists, promotion of an appearance or a quoted source; a word list alone cannot decide. Record theme definitions, inclusion and exclusion examples, disagreement and the rationale for merging or retaining topics. The package creates a worksheet with up to 15 seeded sampled posts per topic and a blank map. This is a starting review packet, not evidence that 15 examples are sufficient for every topic. Researchers should inspect more documents and outliers when interpretation requires it. No final human-reviewed Trump taxonomy is asserted in this release.

2.5 Validate and classify documents

The dissertation describes Boolean and LLM classification pilots and reports selecting GPT-4o after greater than 85% agreement with 250 human-labeled classifications during prompt engineering (pp. 136–137 and 161). Its rationale draws on transformer text-classification research (Fields et al., 2024). The original benchmark labels and confusion matrix are not supplied. The historical classification prompt uses one completion, temperature 0.5 and a 30-token maximum. The maintained implementation allows 120 output tokens to avoid truncating long theme names; this change must be reported when used.

For new research, separate prompt development from held-out evaluation. Report positive support, precision, sensitivity (recall), confusion counts and overall agreement for every theme. Precision is the fraction of predicted positives that are correct; sensitivity is the fraction of human-labeled positives detected. The maintained implementation compares Boolean and LLM agreement, selects the higher-scoring candidate (LLM on a tie), and applies the validation gate before scaling. Overall accuracy alone can conceal failure on a rare category. The maintained gate requires agreement strictly above the chosen threshold and positive benchmark support with nonzero sensitivity for every theme. These minimum checks do not establish that error rates are acceptable for a particular research question.

The maintained Boolean comparator forms each theme dictionary from the representative words of its mapped topics, counts matches and requires at least two hits; it is a transparent baseline, not a reconstruction of the original undisclosed Boolean dictionaries. The LLM assigns one agreed theme or the explicit residual option. Accept only a single unambiguous option. Refusals, multiple choices, failed calls and missing text are unresolved measurements, not evidence that a document belongs to no theme. Do not estimate prevalence until these cases have been resolved.

2.6 Estimate prevalence and compare periods

For theme t in period p, prevalence is k(t,p) / n(p): the number of classified documents assigned to that theme divided by all eligible, successfully classified documents in the period. The historical periods are January 2019–January 2020, March–May 2020 and June 2020–July 2021. February 2020 is outside these comparison periods. Period definitions must be non-overlapping, dates valid, and denominators explicitly reported. As a published illustration, Alcohol Delivery and Isolation Drinking accounted for 9.1% of posts in the pre-period and 17.4% in the peri-period, an increase of 8.3 percentage points (dissertation Table 3, p. 166). Those rounded percentages illustrate the estimand; a new run computes its own counts and denominators before testing differences.

Use a pooled, two-sided, two-proportion z-test for each of the three pairwise comparisons per theme, matching the reported Stata 17 design (StataCorp, 2021). With five themes, the Bonferroni threshold is 0.05 / 15 = 0.003333… (Bland & Altman, 1995). This implementation defines positive z as an increase from the earlier listed period to the later one. Report counts, denominators, percentage-point differences, relative changes, z, p and the comparison family. Zero baseline prevalence makes relative change undefined; zero pooled variance makes the z-test not estimable. Sparse cells need appropriate methodological review.

For periods a and b, define the observed proportions and their pooled estimate:

pa = ka/na; pb = kb/nb
ppool = (ka + kb)/(na + nb)
z = (pb − pa) / √[ppool(1 − ppool)(1/na + 1/nb)]

The two-sided p-value uses the standard normal distribution; apply the declared Bonferroni threshold to that value.

These tests condition on the assigned labels and the independent-binomial approximation. They do not propagate classification uncertainty or account for repeated posts from the same brand and temporal dependence. The estimates describe the sampled corpus, and the comparisons do not establish a causal effect of the pandemic.

3. Exact prompts and implementation contract

The complete prompt templates appear below. Braces denote substituted values. The naming domain for the tutorial is posts from the Donald Trump account archive, June 16, 2015 to January 8, 2021, including retweets; describe content neutrally. The words are the selected topic’s comma-separated representative words. For research classification, N is the number of approved themes plus one residual option; numbered_options joins labels with comma-space separators, followed by the exact residual The text does not fit into any of these themes. The document is the original post text. Theme definitions and examples should be documented in the codebook; any extension of this exact prompt to include them is a new, declared prompt requiring fresh evaluation.

The historical source identifies GPT-4o without establishing a dated snapshot. Maintained defaults pin gpt-4o-2024-08-06, MiniLM revision 1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41, and seed 42 for parameter draws and UMAP. Naming uses temperature 0.5 and 15 maximum output tokens; research classification uses temperature 0.5 and 120 maximum output tokens, increased from the historical 30 to accommodate long labels. The provider is asked not to store completions. Preserve actual provider metadata, prompts and installed dependency versions. API availability and outputs can change despite a model identifier. The archived supplements specify their historical SDK; the maintained package uses the current client interface.

Naming system
You are an expert at naming topics.

Naming user
The following words represent a topic derived from {domain}: {words}. Please provide a concise, meaningful topic name that clearly summarizes these words.
Classification system
You are an expert at classifying text into themes.

Classification user
Classify the following text into one of the following {N} themes: {numbered_options}.Text: {document}.Respond with verbatim classification using only the specified {N} options.

Adapting the workflow to another domain requires more than changing one prompt clause. Reconsider corpus sampling, preprocessing and stopwords, topic interpretation, theme definitions, dictionaries, human benchmarks, period boundaries and inferential assumptions. Retain the residual category, and document how ambiguous or multi-theme documents are handled under the single-label design.

Checkpoint reuse must be bound to a fingerprint of ordered inputs, topic words, theme options, prompts, model and decoding settings. Reordering a corpus or changing a topic’s words invalidates prior assignments even when the row count or topic ID is unchanged. Keep raw responses separate from accepted labels and expose all unresolved outcomes.

4. Worked transfer: the Trump account archive

4.1 Question, source and unit of analysis

The tutorial asks a deliberately descriptive question: what recurring language structures appear in the selected account’s posts across its campaign and first-term activity, and how might researchers turn these structures into interpretable categories? It is not designed to estimate voter attitudes, public opinion, persuasive effects or causal relationships. A posting event is the unit of analysis. Retweets represent amplification by the account; account activity is not equivalent to personally authored statements.

The source is CodeBreaker619’s Donald Trump Tweets Dataset, Kaggle version 1, updated January 10, 2021 (CodeBreaker619, 2021). Starting from its 56,571-record tweets.csv, retain every record with a source date on or after June 16, 2015. Sort by timestamp, then integer ID. No political, sentiment, engagement or language filter is applied. This yields 34,416 distinct IDs, with first timestamp 2015-06-16 07:04:03 and last timestamp 2021-01-08 15:44:28. The archive therefore ends before the first presidential term ended on January 20. The date window follows the specified archive endpoint; it is not a claim of complete capture through the full constitutional term.

The canonical corpus preserves source text, IDs, timestamps, retweet/deletion/flag metadata, device and archived favorites/retweet counts. It contains 9,852 retweets, 1,092 records marked deleted, 343 repeated-text events beyond a first occurrence and no blank raw texts. Repeated text is retained when attached to distinct source IDs, because the unit is a posting event. Metadata flags and engagement counts are the archive’s values, not independently verified or current measurements. The source timestamps have no timezone offset; no conversion is applied, and selection uses the supplied calendar date. Missing or unobserved posts cannot be reconstructed from this file.

4.2 From one corpus to inspectable model output

The reference discovery run uses the search space in Section 2.2, fifty parameter draws, ceilings 2–25, seed 42 and fit-once/reduce mode. Preprocessing leaves 32,725 usable modeled texts; 1,691 source posts have no remaining tokens and are recorded separately. These are modeling eligibility outcomes, not a second dataset. The raw corpus retains all 34,416 records. Each candidate reports its requested ceiling, observed topic count, coherence, outlier share and status. The fitted winning candidate is saved and reloaded to verify its recorded assignments.

The recorded run selected 3 non-outlier topics at cv = 0.5898 (iteration 43, requested ceiling 4). It assigned 21,089 modeled posts to topics and left 11,636 as outliers (35.6% of modeled texts). Candidate statuses were insufficient_topics: 49; scored: 1,151. The saved fitted model’s assignments were verified after reload. These are measured discovery outcomes, not human-approved themes or classifier validation. The downloadable execution record includes all candidates, assignments, exclusions, environment and hashes. No target topic count was imposed.

The selected solution is highly imbalanced: the largest group contains 21,073 posts, while the remaining groups contain 10 and 6. Close reading shows a broad dominant group and small event-specific lexical groups. The unweighted mean coherence gives each topic equal weight regardless of its size, so small internally similar groups can raise the score. This result is useful diagnostic evidence, not a finished taxonomy. Before research classification, review corpus coverage, repetition and alternative candidate settings; record any revised selection rule as a new analysis.

Interactive naming collects a visitor-selected 1, 5, 10 or 20 independent responses. This makes model variation inspectable within a small allowance; it is a teaching adaptation of the historical 5,000-ballot protocol. A visitor may group topics into a local draft and export it, but the export explicitly records that no formal researcher approval has occurred. Public classification examples use declared practice categories to expose the mechanics of a bounded model choice. They are not a validated Trump research taxonomy and are not used to fabricate full-corpus prevalence.

The tutorial’s period chart summarizes recorded topic assignments, using all source posts in each period as its stated denominator and retaining outliers and texts unavailable to discovery as separate outcomes. This is descriptive model coverage, not prevalence of human-validated themes. The research path continues with actual synthesis and independent annotations in the researcher’s fork. A tutorial can show the entire chain without pretending to have completed those scientific decisions.

5. Reproducible execution with agents and researchers

The Python package is the maintained execution environment. The website introduces concepts and exposes reviewable examples. A coding agent reads the method, checks the corpus, executes specified Python functions, retains artifacts and prepares questions for the researcher. It must not substitute a generated narrative for a computed result, fabricate annotations or approve its own synthesis. This separation makes routine work executable without delegating scientific accountability to the model.

Python 3.11 or later is required. In a cloned repository and isolated environment, install the core package with python -m pip install ".[dev]", then run topicllm --profile quickcheck --output-dir runs/quickcheck. This verifies corpus bytes, IDs, coverage and counts with no model or paid calls. Install the ML dependencies with python -m pip install ".[ml]". Run topicllm --profile trump-demo --output-dir runs/trump for discovery; add --plan to inspect the configuration first. The initial run may download the embedding model and NLTK resources. Dependency locks are platform-specific; record actual installed versions.

For a small naming exercise, use topicllm --profile research --stages 2,3 --votes 20 --output-dir runs/trump --plan. Execute only after configuring private provider credentials and an approved --budget-usd. Twenty votes is an explicitly reduced teaching budget, not equivalence to 5,000 votes. The default 5,000-ballot setting can require 125,000 calls for 25 topics; an explicit budget and call ceiling may stop it. Plan actual counts before increasing limits. Paid operations retain a persistent ledger, raw responses and fingerprints; completed matching operations can resume without recharging. Uncertain requests retain conservative reservations rather than assuming zero cost.

After Stage 3, researchers complete the map and theme definitions and set the agreed themes in a JSON configuration. The example configuration intentionally starts with an empty list. Record actual approval with --record-review --reviewers, naming the real reviewers only after their review. The record binds corpus, assignments, topic words, naming evidence, taxonomy and map by hash. It is an attestation, not independent identity verification. Stage 4 requires this current record and a separately annotated CSV containing tweet and human_label. Benchmark metrics are saved even when the gate fails; failed validation is not permission to weaken the threshold.

Run research Stage 4 with the approved configuration, independent benchmark and planned budget. Once review and classification gates pass, Stage 5 computes counts and comparisons from accepted labels. The software blocks unresolved classifications and invalid or overlapping period definitions. A researcher must still assess sparse cells, dependence, uncertainty and substantive scope. For a new corpus, --input selects one source for discovery and classification; a separate --discovery-input is available only when the new design explicitly requires it. Changing data, prompts, taxonomy or settings requires a new declared run rather than silently reusing stale outputs.

The Cloudflare Worker keeps credentials server-side and restricts public examples to approved tutorial records. 6. Limitations, scientific status and availability

Method transfer is not substantive replication. The Trump corpus differs from the alcohol-brand studies in population, period, genre, authorship, posting rate and political context. Its known public subject makes the tutorial approachable but does not remove selection bias or make language categories self-evident. Retweets, repeated text and one account’s temporal dependence can strongly shape clusters. Preprocessing may suppress URLs, named accounts, negation or nuanced context. Topics need close reading, and a high coherence score can reward repetitive vocabulary rather than useful concepts.

LLMs carry shared training biases and may interpret political language inconsistently or reproduce claims without verification. Repeated naming calls do not eliminate these biases. Single-label classification simplifies posts with multiple meanings. Benchmark composition, rare-theme support and annotation disagreement affect measured performance; choosing and tuning a classifier on the same benchmark can overstate generalization. Independent evaluation and sensitivity analyses remain necessary. Simple z-tests do not propagate classifier uncertainty or correct serial dependence; descriptive changes in this account’s posting are not causal effects or population opinions.

Fixed seeds and pinned revisions support repeatability in a recorded environment. They do not guarantee identical results across hardware, dependencies or changing hosted models. Preserve every failed candidate and unresolved call as well as successful outputs. The method’s credibility depends on inspectable decisions, evidence and limits, not on recreating a favored topic count or polished narrative.

The original Study 1 also used LIWC to describe cognitive, emotional and excitative language (Chung & Pennebaker, 2012; Boyd et al., 2022), informed by the Differential Susceptibility to Media Effects Model (Valkenburg & Peter, 2013). Those analyses and Study 2’s exposure-index/GSEM models remain documented in the dissertation; they are outside this package’s topic-to-prevalence scope. The dissertation and study papers are preserved unchanged as research examples, not rewritten to fit the new tutorial.

The studies were revised and accepted under dissertation committee supervision, distinct from journal peer review. The study editions and this maintained methods paper are preprints. TopicLLM has not yet been independently replicated by other researchers. Data-integrity checks, software tests and a measured tutorial run establish specific implementation properties, not independent scientific validity. The release invites evaluation, criticism, replication and extension.

Code, public tutorial data, source papers and execution instructions are available at github.com/jethomasphd/TopicLLM; the visual tutorial and downloads are at topicllm.dev.
