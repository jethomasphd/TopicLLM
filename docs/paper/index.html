TopicLLM: From Campaign Topics to Presidential Theme Prevalence

A human-guided, agent-executable methodology applied to the Donald Trump Twitter archive

Jacob E. Thomas

Correspondence: jethomasphd@gmail.com

Abstract

Topic models reveal recurring language, but using their output as a scientific measurement requires interpretation, an operational codebook, and independent evaluation. TopicLLM connects these tasks in an auditable workflow: transformer-based topic discovery, repeated large language model naming, human theme synthesis, held-out classifier validation, and prevalence estimation. The method originated in dissertation research at The University of Texas at Austin; the original research materials are preserved separately. This paper develops a standalone application to the Donald Trump account archive. Topics are discovered in 7,805 campaign-period posts dated June 16, 2015 through November 8, 2016. Researchers interpret the campaign evidence and freeze a theme codebook. A classifier is selected on development annotations and evaluated against separately annotated presidential posts before being applied to the 26,239 posts dated January 20, 2017 through the archive’s end on January 8, 2021. The primary estimand is each campaign-derived theme’s overall share of presidential posting events. We specify the sampling boundaries, search space, prompts, researcher decisions, validation gates, denominators, and executable Python mechanisms. Agents execute scripts and preserve evidence; humans define and assess the constructs. The accompanying website makes this sequence inspectable, while the repository supports implementation on other text corpora. Campaign discovery, 6,000 naming responses and two-human synthesis are complete: the reviewers retained 12 distinct themes and defined their temporal transfer. Two researchers independently annotated 100 development posts (85% agreement; κ = 0.809) and adjudicated 15 differences. The LLM achieved 64% development agreement with consensus, compared with 30% for a Boolean baseline. These results expose unresolved measurement boundaries; held-out evaluation remains unopened and no presidential prevalence estimates are reported.

Keywords: computational content analysis; topic modeling; thematic interpretation; large language models; human-in-the-loop; temporal transfer; reproducible research

1. Introduction

A researcher studying thousands of short posts needs to answer two different questions: what recurring meanings appear in the text, and how often do defined meanings occur? A topic model helps organize the first task. It does not, by itself, provide a validated measure for the second. The gap between an attractive topic visualization and a defensible prevalence estimate is where interpretive decisions, annotation work, and classification errors become consequential.

TopicLLM makes that transition explicit. A topic is a cluster of texts with a statistical representation. A topic name is a proposed description of that representation. A theme is a concept defined through human interpretation of the documents. A classification is an operational decision that assigns a document to a defined theme. Finally, prevalence is the proportion of eligible documents receiving that classification. Each object has different evidence requirements; the software preserves the transitions between them.

This approach builds on the Theme and Topic system’s integration of topic modeling with qualitative research practice (Gillies et al., 2022). It extends the practical chain from exploratory clusters through a frozen codebook to independently evaluated measurement. The contribution is an executable research protocol with evidence artifacts and explicit stopping points. Repeated model responses assist naming; they do not replace contextual reading or create independent expert consensus.

The Trump archive provides a familiar, temporally ordered application. Prior research examines Twitter as a campaign communication channel and a site of political self-presentation (Enli, 2017). Here the account is a worked example for constructing and evaluating thematic measurements, without assuming that any cluster corresponds to a political construct in advance. Our question is: Which themes can researchers develop from campaign-period posts, and what proportion of subsequent presidential posts expresses each of those themes? Discovery is restricted to the campaign. Presidential text enters the workflow during classifier development, evaluation, and measurement. This separation makes temporal transfer visible: a category useful during an election may be ambiguous, uncommon, or absent during governing, while new subjects may fall outside the campaign codebook.

The primary output is overall prevalence during the presidency through archive end. The unit is an archived posting event, including an account’s retweets. The analysis concerns the content and amplification activity of this account. It does not estimate voter attitudes, individual exposure, persuasion, or the causal effects of political communication. Throughout, “Twitter” and “tweet” identify the historical source and its fields; the implementation accepts other text corpora with an appropriately specified design.

2. Corpus and research design

2.1 One archive, two analytic roles

The source is CodeBreaker619’s Donald Trump Tweets Dataset, version 1, published on Kaggle in January 2021. The release includes one canonical CSV containing the 34,416 source records dated June 16, 2015 or later. Its last recorded date is January 8, 2021, preceding the constitutional end of the term. Dates are taken from the source timestamp without timezone conversion because the archive supplies no timezone offset. The deterministic preparation script preserves the selected text, identifiers, timestamps, retweet and deletion flags, device, and archived engagement metadata.

Each source ID occurs once. Repeated text attached to distinct IDs remains a separate posting event: repetition and retweeting are part of the account’s communication activity. The archive includes staff-mediated posting, retweets, and records flagged as deleted; it is not a verified inventory of personally authored statements. Engagement values describe the archive’s capture, not current engagement. The source cannot establish the completeness of missing or removed posts.

scripts/prepare-trump-data.py records the source selection and file hashes. sampling.read_selected creates the campaign and presidency views without modifying the source file. It preserves IDs as strings, rejects duplicate IDs and invalid dates, verifies non-overlapping windows, and records the selected ordered-ID hash. An invalid date is an error requiring resolution, not a record silently lost at a filter boundary.

2.2 Decisions made before interpretation

The research configuration declares the temporal windows, stopwords, embedding model and revision, hyperparameter search, seeds, naming budget, classifier settings, evaluation threshold, and output directory. Campaign-derived themes are fixed before held-out presidential evaluation. The current application uses two actual human researchers for synthesis and annotation review, and a total OpenAI ceiling of US$100. Paid operations are logged across stages and resumed calls. The dollar ceiling limits execution; reaching it is not a reason to omit difficult records or report partial classification as complete prevalence.

An earlier exploratory run used the combined archive for topic discovery. That run is retained as a prior diagnostic and is outside the present temporal-transfer design. The campaign search is a new declared analysis, not a relabeling of the earlier clusters. Parameters or models changed after inspecting campaign results must be identified as exploratory revisions. Presidential outcomes must not be used to select a campaign model that produces a preferred prevalence pattern.

3. Discover and interpret campaign topics

3.1 Preprocessing and embeddings

stage1_topic_discovery.py first selects the campaign view, then decodes HTML entities, removes URLs and @handles, replaces nonalphabetic characters with spaces, lowercases, tokenizes, removes stopwords, and lemmatizes with NLTK (Bird et al., 2009). The declared custom list is rt, https, http, co, amp, realdonaldtrump, combined with the installed NLTK English list. The complete effective list is saved because NLTK resources can change between environments.

Documents empty after preprocessing are logged and excluded from discovery only. Their original source records remain intact. Removing handles, punctuation, numerals, and English stopwords can suppress politically relevant distinctions, including negation; this is a substantive limitation of the representation. Topic interpretation therefore uses original posts alongside the modeled text. Presidential classification also uses original text, rather than the stripped discovery representation.

SentenceTransformer all-MiniLM-L6-v2 converts the preprocessed campaign texts into embeddings. The model is pinned to revision 1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41. Sentence-BERT provides the sentence-embedding framework (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019), while MiniLM describes the compact transformer architecture (Wang et al., 2020). Embeddings are computed once and reused across candidate clusterings.

3.2 Model search and candidate evidence

BERTopic combines embedding-based clustering with class-based TF-IDF representations of topic words (Grootendorst, 2022). UMAP reduces the embeddings using cosine distance (McInnes et al., 2018); HDBSCAN clusters the reduced coordinates using Euclidean distance and excess-of-mass selection (Campello et al., 2013). HDBSCAN outliers retain label −1 and are not treated as an additional substantive topic. A CountVectorizer and ClassTfidfTransformer identify the representative words used for coherence evaluation and naming.

The search samples 50 configurations, following the practical rationale for randomized hyperparameter search (Bergstra & Bengio, 2012). UMAP neighbors are drawn from 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, and 35; components from 3 through 10; minimum distance from 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, and 0.5. HDBSCAN minimum cluster size is drawn from 5 through 35 in increments of five. Seed 42 controls both parameter draws and UMAP. Each base clustering is fitted once and independently reduced to maximum-topic settings 2 through 25, yielding 1,200 planned candidate evaluations. Each reduction starts from the same base fit, avoiding cumulative reductions across settings.

The numerical selection objective is Gensim’s cv coherence (Röder et al., 2015). Candidates with fewer than two represented topics or nonfinite coherence cannot win. Every attempted setting, failure, coherence score, observed topic count, outlier share, topic size, and largest-topic share is retained. A requested topic ceiling differs from an observed topic count; no particular number of topics is imposed as a result.

The highest-coherence fitted candidate is preserved without a new randomized fit. Coherence is a lexical diagnostic, not proof of interpretability: evaluation research identifies important gaps between automated coherence and applied human judgments (Doogan & Buntine, 2021; Hoyle et al., 2021). Researchers inspect the selected solution’s topic words, sizes, original documents, and outliers before adopting it. A solution dominated by one heterogeneous cluster and several tiny lexical fragments may be unsuitable even when its coherence is high. Any alternative selection must identify the candidate and the substantive rationale; the numerical winner remains in the evidence record.

3.3 Repeated naming as an interpretive aid

stage2_democratic_naming.py asks the pinned OpenAI model gpt-4o-2024-08-06 to name each topic from its representative words and the campaign context. Each request uses temperature 0.5, one completion, and a 32-token output limit (OpenAI, 2026). The run configuration declares the number of calls per topic before execution. The application uses 500 completed calls per adopted topic. An initial 15-token attempt was stopped after truncated responses exhausted its retries. That attempt and a subsequent prompt-encoding correction are retained separately. Every topic is named again using one exact UTF-8 prompt and the same 32-token ceiling; the final tallies do not mix settings. Twelve bounded workers reduce elapsed time. All attempts remain in the cost ledger. An expansion or rerun must be declared rather than used to select a preferred name.

System
You are an expert at naming topics.

User template
The following words represent a topic derived from {domain}: {words}. Please provide a concise, meaningful topic name that clearly summarizes these words.

The domain names Donald Trump account posts from the 2015–2016 campaign and requests neutral description. Completed raw responses and provider metadata are retained with an attempt ledger. Empty or truncated responses are not accepted as naming votes; retries remain within the declared budget. When a failed gateway response omits token usage or raw output, the ledger retains its conservative reservation rather than inventing either value. Surrounding quotes, case, and terminal periods are normalized for the tally. The modal name is the most frequent valid response; its share uses valid responses as the denominator. The runner-up, vote margin, and wording variants help researchers assess ambiguity. Distinct synonyms may split the vote. A high modal share measures consistency under one prompt and model, not correctness or agreement among independent human experts.

3.4 Human synthesis and the frozen codebook

stage3_synthesis_worksheet.py assembles representative words, proposed names, vote summaries, and up to 15 seeded sampled original posts per topic as an initial review packet. Researchers read additional posts wherever that packet is insufficient and inspect outliers for systematic omissions. The Theme and Topic approach informs the connection between computational organization and qualitative judgment (Gillies et al., 2022); an explicit decision trail supports the interpretive work (Nowell et al., 2017).

The author proposes definitions and groupings, and a human research assistant serves as the second reviewer, critiquing the interpretations. Both consider the political and temporal context, retweet attribution, quoted speech, overlaps, and counterexamples. They may merge topics that express the same concept, retain distinct meanings, or decline to interpret an incoherent cluster. These are actual researcher decisions. Neither an LLM-generated worksheet nor an agent’s agreement is counted as human review.

The resulting codebook contains each theme’s name, definition, inclusion and exclusion rules, and examples. It also states how to choose one primary theme when a post contains several meanings, and when to use the residual option. Topic-to-theme mappings are stored in stage3/theme_map.csv; the operational definitions are stored in a JSON codebook. review.py binds the researcher attestation to the source, date windows, assignments, naming output, map, and codebook hashes. Changing that evidence invalidates approval.

4. Validate measurement in presidential posts

4.1 Human annotations and distinct evaluation roles

Temporal separation of discovery does not guarantee successful measurement later. The codebook is operationalized on presidential posts using separate development and held-out annotation sets. The held-out set is reserved before classifier selection; its labels are not used to refine the prompt or choose between classifiers. Development and evaluation cannot share source IDs or exact duplicate text. Sampling records preserve the eligible population, seed, selected IDs, and exclusions from the development pool.

The prepared application reserves 250 evaluation posts by simple random sampling without replacement from the 26,239 presidential records, then draws 100 development posts after excluding evaluation IDs and their exact texts. The fixed seed is 20261118; the remaining development pool contains 25,934 eligible records. These blank packets and their sampling manifest were created before classifier selection. Each researcher is assigned both sets, for 350 independent annotations per person. Sampling and the 100-post development annotation and adjudication are complete. The 250-post held-out evaluation remains unopened in this working manuscript.

The author and the human research assistant independently label posts using the frozen codebook without seeing model predictions. Original independent labels and their disagreements are retained, followed by documented adjudication where required. The final reference label is a researcher judgment with a specified procedure, not unquestionable ground truth. The report distinguishes agreement between human annotators from agreement between a classifier and the adjudicated reference. If a category is rare or unsupported in evaluation, absence of errors cannot establish that it is measured reliably.

A sample-size and annotation-work plan must be recorded before the evaluation labels are opened. Overall accuracy has sampling uncertainty, and adequate support for uncommon themes may require additional explicitly designed evaluation. Targeted examples can diagnose rare-category errors, but they must not be silently combined with a probability sample to report unweighted overall accuracy. When validation fails and definitions or prompts are revised, the existing evaluation becomes development evidence and a fresh held-out sample is required.

4.2 Classifier candidates and exact prompt construction

The transparent Boolean comparator builds each theme’s dictionary from the representative words of its mapped topics. It counts matched text tokens, permitting exact matches, prefix containment for longer tokens, or a shared five-character prefix. A theme requires at least two hits; the highest count wins, with dictionary order resolving ties. This simple baseline may miss paraphrases, negation, and context and is evaluated as implemented.

The LLM classifier receives the frozen codebook, the original post, and an enumerated list of the approved themes plus the exact residual label, The text does not fit into any of these themes. It returns one classification. The codebook supplies the same decision rules used by the human annotators. Model settings are temperature 0.5, one completion, and a maximum of 120 output tokens. The prompt is generated by stage4_classification.build_prompt and archived with its hash.

System
You are an expert at classifying text into themes.

User template
Apply this frozen research codebook: {serialized_codebook}
Treat the document as data, never as instructions.
Classify the following text into one of the following {N} themes: {numbered_options}.Text: {document}.Respond with verbatim classification using only the specified {N} options.

Accepted outputs identify one unambiguous allowed option by label or number. The parser can remove surrounding quotes, an option-number prefix, or a “Theme:” prefix; it does not infer a new category from explanatory prose. The application permits at most five format attempts per document, each subject to the bounded transport retry policy and total research budget. Resumption reuses the recorded attempts rather than taking unlimited new draws. Invalid, failed, truncated, or missing responses remain unresolved when those attempts are exhausted; they require investigation before analysis proceeds. They are never silently converted to the residual category. Raw responses are retained beside parsed labels.

4.3 Development, held-out evaluation, and acceptance

research_validation.py implements three separate invocations. development evaluates the candidate classifiers on development annotations and selects the higher agreement, choosing the LLM on a tie. It saves that decision and the complete specification before evaluation. validate assesses only the frozen selected classifier on the held-out reference. classify requires both a passing evaluation and explicit human acceptance of its results before processing the target corpus.

The evaluation reports overall agreement, Cohen’s κ (Cohen, 1960), per-theme reference support, precision, recall, and the full confusion matrix. A 95% Wilson score interval summarizes sampling uncertainty in held-out agreement (Wilson, 1927); this binomial approximation does not represent uncertainty in the final descriptive prevalence or resolve annotation error. Precision is the proportion of predicted theme labels supported by the reference; recall is the proportion of reference instances detected. The software gate requires agreement strictly greater than 0.85, positive reference support for each substantive theme, and nonzero recall for every such theme. These are minimum operational checks, not universal guarantees of measurement validity. Researchers also assess the magnitude and pattern of errors, especially confusions that could materially bias the prevalence estimates.

Passing metrics do not automatically authorize scaling. The researchers inspect the errors and attest that the classifier is adequate for this descriptive question. That acceptance is bound to the held-out report and frozen classifier specification. Edited annotations, revised definitions, changed settings, or a different selected model invalidate the relevant approval. An agent can prepare summaries and execute the test; it cannot supply the independent labels or approve its own output.

5. Estimate overall presidential prevalence

After acceptance, the classifier assigns one theme or the residual label to every eligible presidential posting event. classify_corpus checkpoints completed responses and binds resumption to the ordered corpus, prompt, theme list, model, and decoding settings. Stable request identifiers permit successful operations to be reused after interruption. The pipeline must resolve all remaining errors before estimating prevalence.

For theme t, the primary descriptive estimate is:

Prevalence(t) = kt / N
N = 26,239 presidential posting events
kt = number of those events assigned to theme t

stage5_inference.py verifies that classifications exhaust the declared target IDs exactly. It writes the numerator, common denominator, and share for every approved theme, including zero-count categories, and for the residual category. The residual is reported alongside substantive themes so the taxonomy’s coverage remains visible. No campaign posts or transition posts enter the presidential denominator.

These estimates describe classifier-assigned shares of the archived presidential corpus. They are conditional on the codebook and measured classification performance. They do not remove false positives or false negatives, recover missing archive records, or measure every possible theme arising after the campaign. A substantial residual share may reflect new subjects, insufficient textual information, or limitations in the definitions; close reading is needed to distinguish these explanations.

The application reports overall presidency prevalence only. Repeated posts from one account are temporally and substantively dependent, and the archive is not a random sample of a broader political population. Accordingly, the primary report makes no independent-binomial population inference or causal claim. Classification-error diagnostics accompany the descriptive estimates; a later error-corrected prevalence analysis would require its own identifiable assumptions and adequate reference data.

6. Executable research and reporting

6.1 Human decisions, agent execution

The repository is designed to be forked or installed as a Python package. The researcher begins with the visual walkthrough and research question, then provides an agent with the configuration and maintained instructions. The agent invokes the Python functions, retains the evidence, and pauses at scientific decisions. It does not replace computed assignments with plausible summaries or synthesize nonexistent annotation records. The website’s chat guide explains the public method and sources; the researcher’s own environment performs the research run.

Budget exhaustion stops work without fabricating the missing result. Mock backends are labeled engineering fixtures and cannot supply research findings.

6.3 Human synthesis and temporal transfer

On November 18, 2026, Jacob E. Thomas and a human research assistant completed interpretation and consensus review. The first reviewer judged the 12 topics sufficiently distinct to retain separately. The second reviewer questioned the transfer of campaign-specific and one-off categories, the breadth of the immigration category, and the treatment of media criticism. The resulting codebook retains 12 themes (Table 4), with revised definitions and boundaries rather than forced consolidation. The software supports many-to-one topic mappings; the absence of mergers in this application is a documented interpretive decision.

The consensus rules broaden several campaign-specific meanings for application to the presidency. The Republican-primary theme encompasses criticism of Republican actors and former allies; rallies include continuing presidential rallies; immigration is interpreted alongside nationalism; media criticism can enter political opposition; and retail and resort categories admit related later events. These are declared extensions of campaign-derived constructs, not empirical evidence that they transfer successfully. The independent presidential benchmark will test whether people and the frozen classifier can apply those rules consistently. Overlap among political criticism, security and military content is a substantive validation concern.

6.4 Development findings and unresolved measurement boundaries

On November 19, 2026, the author and research assistant completed independent annotation of all 100 development posts. They agreed on 85 posts (Cohen’s κ = 0.809) and jointly adjudicated all 15 differences, retaining both original labels and a written rationale for each resolution. This initial human agreement is distinct from the consensus reference used to assess classifiers; adjudication does not retrospectively make independent agreement perfect.

The prespecified higher-agreement rule selected the LLM, but 64% development agreement does not establish suitability for prevalence measurement. All 100 LLM requests returned parseable labels; this result is not caused by missing calls or invalid outputs. The model selected the residual for 41 posts, compared with 21 consensus residual labels. Of its 36 disagreements with the reference, 24 were model-residual responses to a substantive human label. Eleven of the 38 political-opposition reference posts were missed in this way. All seven consumer-relations posts and the single tax-and-healthcare post were assigned other labels. Three themes had no development reference instances, so their sensitivity cannot be assessed from this sample.

Close reading of development disagreements identifies questions for researcher review, rather than automatically establishing that every model response is wrong. For example, human consumer-relations labels encompass broad employment, manufacturing and economic claims, whereas the written definition emphasizes retail and consumer events. Other differences concern implicit political targets, attribution in retweets, and the primary meaning of short or multi-theme posts. Clarification must align the written rules with the intended construct. Human labels must never be changed merely to agree with a model. Any approved revision will retain this first codebook, annotations and comparison, record its rationale, and obtain renewed human review of affected annotation evidence before further development. The 250-post probability sample remains unavailable to these decisions.

The development report, independent labels and adjudication, raw and parsed predictions and frozen specification retain this result. The development calls used 159,004 prompt tokens and 910 completion tokens, conservatively accounted at US$0.40661 under the recorded price schedule. No held-out or full-target classification was executed.

6.5 Reproducibility and availability

For each release, retain the repository commit and source hashes, Python and package versions, model revisions, effective stopwords, seeds, all candidate results and failures, fitted assignments, exclusions, naming ballots, human decisions, annotation protocol, classifier specification, evaluation errors, final classifications, and API ledger. Dependency environments and available hosted models can change. Seeds support repetition within a recorded environment; they do not guarantee identical clustering or LLM responses across hardware, libraries, or service revisions.

The source archive, preparation manifest, code, documentation, and release artifacts are available at github.com/jethomasphd/TopicLLM. The visual workflow is available at topicllm.dev. Code is MIT-licensed and documentation has separate terms. The Kaggle source designates the data as “Data files © Original Authors”; third-party text is not relicensed by the software release. Public access is not an assertion of unrestricted reuse or complete historical coverage.

7. Discussion

TopicLLM’s central discipline is to preserve the distinction between discovering categories and measuring their occurrence. Temporal transfer makes this discipline consequential: themes constructed from a campaign may offer a useful lens on the presidency, but that lens must be evaluated on presidential language. A clear residual option, independent reference labels, and visible errors allow researchers to judge where the lens works and where it loses coverage.

The workflow has limitations. Preprocessing changes the signal available to discovery; embeddings and clustering choices alter the partitions; coherence can reward lexical regularity over useful concepts. Researcher interpretation introduces judgment rather than eliminating it. Repeated naming calls share a model’s training biases and can be consistently misleading. Hosted LLMs may have encountered the public archive during training, so this application cannot establish generalization to unseen private corpora. Single-label coding compresses posts that express several meanings. Both annotation disagreement and classification error can affect the final estimates.

These limitations motivate the release’s reviewable artifacts and stopping points. The method can be assessed, revised, and implemented in another domain because its decisions are connected to scripts and retained evidence. Independent applications should formulate their own research question, sampling windows, interpretive framework, codebook, validation design, and acceptance criteria. The strongest demonstration is a complete trace from observed text to a cautiously stated measurement, with human judgments documented at the steps where they enter.
