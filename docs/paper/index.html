Coherence-Optimized Topic Modeling with Democratic Large Language Model Interpretation: A Human-in-the-Loop Pipeline for Public Health Surveillance of Social Media

Jacob E. Thomas¹, Dhiraj Murthy², Miguel Pinedo¹, Alexandra Loukas¹, Keryn E. Pasch¹

¹Department of Kinesiology and Health Education, The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, TX, USA

²School of Journalism and Media, The University of Texas at Austin, Austin, TX, USA

Correspondence: jethomasphd@gmail.com

Abstract

Large collections of social media text require computational scale and contextual human interpretation. TopicLLM is a five-stage methodology that connects these requirements: discover topics with coherence-guided BERTopic modeling; propose topic names through repeated large language model (LLM) queries; synthesize themes through researcher review; validate a classifier and apply it to the corpus; and estimate theme prevalence across time. Developed in dissertation research at The University of Texas at Austin, the workflow is illustrated with Study 1, comprising 5,872 posts from 11 alcohol brands between January 2019 and July 2021. The dissertation reported seven topics synthesized into five themes. The released demonstration provides the Study 1 data and a documented modern discovery run, while identifying published findings separately. This paper specifies the preprocessing, search space, prompts, review procedure, validation criteria and statistical comparisons, and connects them to an executable Python workflow. Coding agents run the prescribed scripts and retain evidence; researchers approve interpretation and validation. Probabilistic models and changes in software or model versions can yield different outputs. The method is offered for independent evaluation and adaptation, rather than as an independently validated standard.

Keywords: topic modeling; BERTopic; large language models; human-in-the-loop; social media; public health; reproducible research

1. Introduction

TopicLLM originated in dissertation research at The University of Texas at Austin on alcohol companies’ social media communication during the COVID-19 pandemic (Thomas, 2025). The substantive question was what alcohol companies communicated during the pandemic and how the prevalence of those messages changed. Topic models can organize thousands of posts, but a statistical group does not explain its public health meaning. Qualitative interpretation supplies that context, while requiring a transparent way to connect interpretation to corpus-scale measurement. The Theme and Topic framework integrates topic modeling with qualitative research (Gillies et al., 2022). TopicLLM builds on that approach with an explicit, inspectable sequence linking transformer-based discovery, repeated LLM naming, researcher-led synthesis, validated classification and prevalence estimation. It is offered as a starting implementation for researchers to evaluate and adapt.

Study 1 identified themes in commercial alcohol-brand posts; Study 2 applied a selected theme to a larger corpus of general users’ posts and examined potential exposure to brand messages. The maintained package focuses on the Study 1 topic-to-prevalence workflow. Historical references to Twitter and tweets refer to the platform now called X. Applications to other social media or text corpora require their own sampling rationale, preprocessing, interpretive framework and classifier validation.

The purpose of this workflow is to make a large text corpus analyzable without hiding the interpretive decisions between statistical clusters and meaningful themes. Topics are patterns estimated by a model. Labels describe those patterns. Themes are categories justified by researchers through contextual interpretation, drawing on the qualitative principles discussed in the dissertation (Braun & Clarke, 2006; Nowell et al., 2017). A classifier then applies the agreed taxonomy to documents, providing the numerators for prevalence estimates. Here, a corpus is the collection of texts; a topic is a group inferred from patterns in their language; a theme is a category defined through researcher interpretation; and prevalence is the proportion of eligible texts assigned to a theme. “Democratic” naming denotes a frequency tally of repeated model responses, not consensus among human researchers.

Thomas (2025), Chapter 4 and Appendices 1–3, supplies the historical methods and findings. The present paper specifies the topic-to-prevalence workflow and identifies operational additions in the maintained implementation, including persisted model artifacts, budget controls and explicit researcher and validation checkpoints. It does not require the reader to use the website to understand the method.

This release distinguishes findings reported in the dissertation, a modern illustrative rerun on original Study 1 data, and new researcher-run analyses. The demonstration makes the methodology and evidence inspectable without requiring identical historical outputs. Agents execute the documented workflow; researchers review interpretation and validation.

2. The five-stage method

2.1 Corpus design and preprocessing

Define the population, collection mechanism, inclusion rules and time boundaries before modeling. Retain an immutable source copy, stable document identifiers and a record of exclusions. Study 1 comprised 5,872 tweets from 11 alcohol companies, posted between January 2019 and July 2021 and collected through the UT-Austin Prevention Research Lab Twitter Marketing Study. Brands represented beer, spirits and hard seltzer and had at least 10,000 followers. Between February 2021 and May 2022, trained research assistants audited verified profiles using a standardized entry form, with weekly coordinator quality checks and regular team meetings. The dissertation reports a UT-Austin determination of non-human-subject research (pp. 154–155). Section 4 describes the released demonstration.

The historical implementation used Python 3.9.12 (Python Software Foundation, 2022). Appendix 1 removes non-alphabetic characters, collapses whitespace, lowercases text, tokenizes with NLTK (Bird et al., 2009), removes English and custom domain stopwords, and applies part-of-speech-informed WordNet lemmatization. Domain stopwords include brand terms and temporal words. Document the exact list and operation order; they change the vocabulary and the geometry of the subsequent analysis. Retain an exclusion record for blank or unusable text. Text preprocessing is an analytic step; the demonstration starts from the released Study 1 data and does not require users to reconstruct the collection archive.

2.2 Discover topics

BERTopic was chosen for flexible topic discovery and coherence-guided exploration, consistent with the comparison discussed in the dissertation (Egger & Yu, 2022). The model first converts each preprocessed text into a numeric sentence embedding using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019; Wang et al., 2020). Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) reduces these vectors to a smaller space using cosine distance (McInnes et al., 2018). Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise (HDBSCAN) then groups nearby observations using Euclidean distance and excess-of-mass cluster selection (Campello et al., 2013). Texts not assigned to a cluster remain outliers, labeled −1. BERTopic represents each cluster with characteristic words using class-based term frequency–inverse document frequency (TF-IDF), then can merge topics to evaluate a requested topic ceiling (Grootendorst, 2022). The representation uses CountVectorizer with the same stopword set and ClassTfidfTransformer; class-based weighting is implemented by BERTopic, building on the TF-IDF principle described by Ramos (2003). The random search draws 50 parameter configurations (Bergstra & Bengio, 2012). UMAP neighbors are sampled from 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35; components from 3 through 10; minimum distance from 0.01, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.5; HDBSCAN minimum cluster size from 5 through 35 in increments of five. Each draw considers maximum-topic settings 2 through 25: 1,200 planned candidate fits.

Gensim’s cv coherence, a measure of how strongly a topic’s representative words occur together in the corpus, is the selection objective (Röder et al., 2015). The dissertation motivates coherence through research on human interpretability (Newman et al., 2010) and a BERTopic application to e-cigarette social media content (Lee et al., 2024). Record attempted, successful and failed candidates separately. A requested maximum-topic setting is not the observed number of non-outlier topics. Coherence measures word association, not substantive validity, and can favor narrow lexical patterns in narrow or repetitive text. Inspect outlier share, representative documents and sensitivity to preprocessing as well as the selected score.

The dissertation reports iteration 25, UMAP neighbors 15, components 4 and minimum distance 0.01, HDBSCAN minimum cluster size 20, maximum-topic setting 23, cv 0.768, and seven observed topics (p. 163). The maintained implementation retains the fitted candidate that earned the winning score, together with its configuration, assignments and provenance. This preserves the connection between the reported search score and the model used downstream. For each sampled configuration, the maintained search fits the embedding reduction and clustering once, then evaluates topic reductions across ceilings. The archival supplement refits for each ceiling; refit_per_solution=true retains that option. This implementation choice is recorded in the run configuration and may affect results. The modern run uses Study 1 data with a recorded software environment and declared seed. It illustrates the method without claiming identical historical outputs.

2.3 Name topics with repeated LLM queries

The dissertation draws on emerging work on LLM topic labeling (Li et al., 2023). Its naming protocol makes 5,000 separate GPT-4o calls per topic (OpenAI, 2024), each with temperature 0.5, a maximum of 15 output tokens and one completion. It adopts the most frequent interpretation (p. 159 and Appendix 2). Seven topics imply 35,000 naming calls before retries. These are repeated draws from one model, not independent human annotators or an ensemble of independently trained models.

The maintained implementation retains raw ballots and normalizes surrounding quotes, case and terminal periods for tallying. The denominator is the number of valid votes, not attempted calls. Failed, empty or truncated responses must not become labels. Preserve the normalization rule, requested and completed counts, model identifier, decoding parameters and any stopping rule. Ties require explicit inspection. A fragmented vote can indicate synonymous wording as well as semantic ambiguity; a concentrated vote does not establish truth or remove shared model bias.

The optional leader-share stopping rule ends voting after a declared minimum number of votes when the cumulative leading share reaches a threshold (defaults: 500 votes and 0.60). It is disabled by default. It is a cost heuristic introduced by the maintained implementation. It is not a demonstrated convergence test and is not the fixed-budget historical protocol.

2.4 Synthesize themes through researcher review

Two researchers review representative words, sampled documents, model labels and relevant literature, following the Theme and Topic approach and related applied work (Gillies et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2024). In Study 1, interpretation was informed by COVID-19 alcohol-marketing studies from Australia and New Zealand (Martino et al., 2021; Gerritsen et al., 2021). They examine whether labels fit the posts and group topics by shared meaning and context. The primary researcher checks the proposed interpretations; a senior researcher reviews and critiques them; discussion resolves differences (dissertation pp. 159–160). This is a collaborative interpretive procedure rather than blinded independent annotation. Record reviewers, evidence, decisions, exclusions and the final topic-to-theme map.

In the historical study, seven topics became five themes. For example, the words “restaurant,” “donate,” “support” and “worker” supported the topic name “COVID-19 Restaurant Support and Donation Initiatives”; review of the associated posts informed the theme “Restaurant Support” (Table 1). The word list proposes a pattern, and the documents and literature establish its interpretation. The two topics concerning virtual bar tours and social engagement became Social Media Promotions. The virtual draft party and home sports challenge topics became Sports. Delivery, restaurant support and charity gaming each contributed one topic to their respective theme. Topic IDs shown in the illustrative walkthrough are display identifiers, not recovered original model IDs.

2.5 Validate and classify documents

The dissertation describes Boolean and LLM classification pilots and reports selecting GPT-4o after greater than 85% agreement with 250 human-labeled classifications during prompt engineering (pp. 136–137 and 161). Its rationale draws on transformer text-classification research (Fields et al., 2024). The original benchmark labels and confusion matrix are not supplied. The historical classification prompt uses one completion, temperature 0.5 and a 30-token maximum. The maintained implementation allows 120 output tokens to avoid truncating long theme names; this change must be reported when used.

For new research, separate prompt development from held-out evaluation. Report positive support, precision, sensitivity (recall), confusion counts and overall agreement for every theme. Precision is the fraction of predicted positives that are correct; sensitivity is the fraction of human-labeled positives detected. The maintained implementation compares Boolean and LLM agreement, selects the higher-scoring candidate (LLM on a tie), and applies the validation gate before scaling. Overall accuracy alone can conceal failure on a rare category. The maintained gate requires agreement strictly above the chosen threshold and positive benchmark support with nonzero sensitivity for every theme. These minimum checks do not establish that error rates are acceptable for a particular research question.

The maintained Boolean comparator forms each theme dictionary from the representative words of its mapped topics, counts matches and requires at least two hits; it is a transparent baseline, not a reconstruction of the original undisclosed Boolean dictionaries. The LLM assigns one agreed theme or the explicit residual option. Accept only a single unambiguous option. Refusals, multiple choices, failed calls and missing text are unresolved measurements, not evidence that a document belongs to no theme. Do not estimate prevalence until these cases have been resolved.

2.6 Estimate prevalence and compare periods

For theme t in period p, prevalence is k(t,p) / n(p): the number of classified documents assigned to that theme divided by all eligible, successfully classified documents in the period. The historical periods are January 2019–January 2020, March–May 2020 and June 2020–July 2021. February 2020 is outside these comparison periods. Period definitions must be non-overlapping, dates valid, and denominators explicitly reported. As a published illustration, Alcohol Delivery and Isolation Drinking accounted for 9.1% of posts in the pre-period and 17.4% in the peri-period, an increase of 8.3 percentage points (dissertation Table 3, p. 166). Those rounded percentages illustrate the estimand; a new run computes its own counts and denominators before testing differences.

Use a pooled, two-sided, two-proportion z-test for each of the three pairwise comparisons per theme, matching the reported Stata 17 design (StataCorp, 2021). With five themes, the Bonferroni threshold is 0.05 / 15 = 0.003333… (Bland & Altman, 1995). This implementation defines positive z as an increase from the earlier listed period to the later one. Report counts, denominators, percentage-point differences, relative changes, z, p and the comparison family. Zero baseline prevalence makes relative change undefined; zero pooled variance makes the z-test not estimable. Sparse cells need appropriate methodological review.

For periods a and b, let pa = ka/na, pb = kb/nb, and the pooled proportion ppool = (ka + kb)/(na + nb). Then z = (pb − pa) / √[ppool(1 − ppool)(1/na + 1/nb)]. The two-sided p-value uses the standard normal distribution; apply the declared Bonferroni threshold to that value.

These tests condition on the assigned labels and the independent-binomial approximation. They do not propagate classification uncertainty or account for repeated posts from the same brand and temporal dependence. The estimates describe the sampled corpus, and the comparisons do not establish a causal effect of the pandemic.

3. Exact prompts and implementation contract

Human-readable templates appear below. Braces denote substituted values, not literal prompt text. For Study 1, {domain} is “tweets generated by alcohol companies during the COVID-19 pandemic”; {words} is the topic’s comma-separated representative word list. Classification uses six numbered options: the five themes in Table 1, in their listed order, followed by “The text does not fit into any of these themes.” {N} therefore equals 6; {numbered_options} joins the numbered labels with comma-space separators; {document} is the original post text. The machine-readable protocol.json also preserves exact concatenation, spaces and output limits. Pin the actual model identifier and preserve provider-returned metadata where available. The historical source identifies GPT-4o but does not establish the precise dated snapshot used. The maintained defaults pin gpt-4o-2024-08-06, MiniLM revision 1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41, and random seed 42 for parameter draws and UMAP. Appendices 2–3 specify the legacy OpenAI SDK 0.27.0; the maintained package uses the current SDK interface. Record installed versions rather than treating the historical environment as the current installation specification.

Naming system
You are an expert at naming topics.

Naming user
The following words represent a topic derived from {domain}: {words}. Please provide a concise, meaningful topic name that clearly summarizes these words.
Classification system
You are an expert at classifying text into themes.

Classification user
Classify the following text into one of the following {N} themes: {numbered_options}.Text: {document}.Respond with verbatim classification using only the specified {N} options.

Adapting the workflow to another domain requires more than changing one prompt clause. Reconsider corpus sampling, preprocessing and stopwords, topic interpretation, theme definitions, dictionaries, human benchmarks, period boundaries and inferential assumptions. Retain the residual category, and document how ambiguous or multi-theme documents are handled under the single-label design.

Checkpoint reuse must be bound to a fingerprint of ordered inputs, topic words, theme options, prompts, model and decoding settings. Reordering a corpus or changing a topic’s words invalidates prior assignments even when the row count or topic ID is unchanged. Keep raw responses separate from accepted labels and expose all unresolved outcomes.

4. Study 1 demonstration

The demonstration centers on one research dataset: the author-released Study 1 alcohol-brand corpus, containing 5,872 records from 11 brands, January 2019–July 2021. Users begin with this prepared release. Topic discovery uses its configured lockdown-period input; classification and period comparisons use the full-period text input. These are stage-specific inputs for the same Study 1 example. Exact file selections, eligibility rules and hashes are specified in the data specification and execution contract. Study 2 user data are not released.

The recorded search retained 1,200 candidate results: 1,151 were scored and 49 had too few topics. Its selected solution contained five topics, with cv = 0.7803; 433 of 704 texts were left as outliers (61.5%). This limited coverage requires substantive review. These statistical groups are distinct from the dissertation’s five researcher-defined themes. The demo record links to the complete execution evidence.

The walkthrough uses recorded discovery to explain topics, live OpenAI calls to illustrate naming and classification, and dissertation findings to illustrate researcher synthesis and prevalence. Published results are labeled at the point of use. Topic models and LLMs include probabilistic steps; seeds, models and software changes can alter outputs. The demo illustrates the workflow without requiring identical historical results.

The Python package is the research implementation. The optional browser sandbox uses approximate local discovery and simulated LLM responses for learning. New research requires researcher-approved themes and an independent classification benchmark. The original study also described cognitive, emotional and excitative language using LIWC (Chung & Pennebaker, 2012; Boyd et al., 2022), framed by the Differential Susceptibility to Media Effects Model (Valkenburg & Peter, 2013). That additional analysis, and Study 2’s exposure-index/GSEM analysis, are documented in the dissertation but are outside this package’s topic-to-prevalence scope.

5. Reproducible execution with agents and researchers

The website introduces the method; the public repository and Python package are the execution environment. A researcher supplies the question and reviews the scientific decisions. An agent reads AGENTS.md, REPRODUCE.md and protocol.json, checks the original inputs, records the environment, plans costs, runs the stages and assembles their evidence. The quickcheck profile verifies the bundled Study 1 data and configuration without model calls. The study1-demo profile runs topic discovery using the bundled settings. The Fork + run guide provides installation commands and the step-by-step research handoff.

For a new research corpus, explicitly declare the sampling frame, any discovery subset, stopwords, themes, independent benchmark and period boundaries. A corpus CSV must contain a nonempty tweet field, a stable post_id is recommended, and period analysis requires a valid date in YYYY-MM-DD form. The independent benchmark CSV contains tweet and human_label, using the exact agreed theme labels or residual option. For a new corpus, --input supplies both discovery and classification; --discovery-input explicitly selects a separate discovery sample when the research design requires one. Approval records bind the selected data and current synthesis evidence. Paid stages require an explicit budget and private provider credentials.

For research, two actual researchers review and approve the theme map before classification, and the classifier must pass the declared benchmark gate. The interactive public examples illustrate those operations without pretending that a demonstration label is a validated research finding.

Retain input hashes, exact dependency and model revisions, seeds, successful and failed candidates, topic sizes and outliers, raw naming responses, theme decisions, benchmark support and per-theme performance, classifications, exclusions and period denominators. Fixed seeds improve repeatability within a recorded environment; they do not guarantee historical equivalence across changed models, dependencies or hardware.

The Cloudflare Worker keeps credentials server-side and accepts only released Study 1 IDs for live model examples. The conversational guide answers from public research and repository documentation. Both share atomic spending and usage limits. Arbitrary uploaded research records are processed only in the researcher's approved environment. The website offers a transparent demonstration; a fork provides the auditable path to a new analysis.

5.1 Minimal execution recipe

Obtain the code at github.com/jethomasphd/TopicLLM and record its commit. The commands below apply to release 0.4.0, from the repository root after activating an isolated Python 3.11+ environment. The ml extra installs the topic-modeling dependencies. A fresh output directory separates the run from earlier analyses.

Install, verify, and discover (no paid API calls)
python -m pip install ".[ml]"
topicllm --profile quickcheck --output-dir runs/check
topicllm --profile study1-demo --output-dir runs/study1 --plan
topicllm --profile study1-demo --output-dir runs/study1

Inspect the saved topic words, representative texts and outlier coverage. For naming, set OPENAI_API_KEY privately in the execution environment and choose an authorized spending ceiling. This example requests 20 votes per topic to demonstrate the mechanism; reproducing the historical naming schedule requires 5,000 votes per topic, an adequate call ceiling and a separately planned budget.

Name and prepare the researcher review packet
topicllm --profile research --stages 2,3 --votes 20 --budget-usd 5 --output-dir runs/study1

The $5 ceiling is a limit for this run directory, not a cost estimate or an assurance that every request will complete. The agent stops at the review packet. Two researchers inspect the evidence, complete stage3/theme_map.csv and agree on the configured theme definitions. Their names and approval are recorded using --record-review --reviewers; an agent must not supply fictitious reviewers. The record is invalidated if its bound inputs, topic evidence or theme decisions change.

Next, supply the independently annotated benchmark with --benchmark human_labels.csv. Use --stages 4,5 --plan to review classification and inference costs, then execute those stages with the same approved configuration and a sufficient authorized budget. Classification proceeds only after researcher approval and a passing benchmark. Preserve the resulting per-document labels, benchmark metrics, theme counts, period denominators and comparison results alongside the configuration and software environment. The sequence is executable by a coding agent, but its scientific decisions remain researcher responsibilities.

6. Availability, limitations and research use

The source code, Study 1 demonstration data, archival dissertation, study papers, prompts and execution records are available at github.com/jethomasphd/TopicLLM. The visual demonstration and downloadable package are at topicllm.dev. The code is MIT-licensed; material-specific terms are in LICENSE-CONTENT.md, and third-party brand text is not relicensed by the software license. Original papers are protected by checksums. A source-to-method map records the dissertation passages supporting this methods edition.

Transparency means readers can inspect the inputs, preparation, settings, decisions and outputs. Reproducibility here means another agent or researcher can rerun the documented workflow and assess the resulting evidence. It is not a promise that every probabilistic output will be identical to the dissertation. Researchers remain responsible for interpretation, validation and the scope of scientific claims.

The dissertation studies were reviewed, revised and accepted under dissertation committee supervision. This is distinct from journal peer review; the study editions and this maintained methods paper are presented as preprints. The TopicLLM methodology has not yet been independently replicated by other researchers. Software tests, input audits and illustrative reruns verify specific implementation properties but do not establish independent scientific validation. Open release of the sources, code, data and execution records is intended to enable such evaluation, criticism and extension. The author’s welcome explains this invitation and the intended researcher-and-agent workflow.
